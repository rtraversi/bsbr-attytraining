Iurix Accreditation trains law firm staff on the responsible use of artificial intelligence. Because
we sell a product *about* AI supervision, we think you are entitled to a precise account of what role
AI plays in the Service itself.

We have written this in plain language rather than legal hedging, because the answer is
straightforward and we would rather just say it.

> ### The short version
>
> **The certification pathway contains no artificial intelligence.** Assessment questions are written
> by people and fixed in advance. Scoring is deterministic and happens on our servers. No AI model
> reads your answers, grades your work, or decides whether you pass. We never use your data to train
> AI models, and we never will without your express written consent.

## 1. Where AI Does — and Does Not — Appear

### The assessment contains no AI

This is the part that determines whether a certificate is issued, so it is the part that matters most.

- **Questions are fixed.** They are drawn from a bank written and reviewed by people. Nothing is
  generated at the time you sit the assessment.
- **Scoring is deterministic.** Each question has one correct answer recorded in advance. Your score
  is the count of correct answers against the pass threshold. The same answers always produce the
  same result.
- **Scoring happens on our servers.** A result submitted by a browser is never trusted. This is a
  security measure, not an AI one, but it is part of why the result is reliable.
- **No model interprets your answers.** There is no free-text grading, no semantic scoring, no
  language model in the path between your submission and your certificate.

If someone asks whether an AI decided you were qualified, the answer is no. A human-written answer
key did.

### The training content contains no AI

The course is a fixed, pre-authored interactive package. It presents the same material to every
learner. It does not adapt, personalise, generate, or respond using AI.

### Course authoring

**No generative AI was used to create this course.**

The curriculum was designed by a licensed attorney, who developed and approved the content outline.
The course material was then authored by a member of our team working from that approved outline.
Every lesson, scenario, and assessment question in the Service was written by a person.

We state this plainly because a course that teaches attorneys to supervise AI-generated work should
be able to account for its own provenance.

## 2. What We Do and Do Not Do With Your Data

**We do:**

- Process your training and assessment data solely to operate the Service and issue your certificate
- Keep each firm's records separated from every other firm's at the database layer
- Use aggregate, non-identifying operational data to monitor performance and reliability

**We do not:**

- Use your data, your staff's data, or your assessment responses to train AI models — ever
- Share your data with any third-party AI provider for training or any other purpose
- Sell or license your data to anyone
- Use AI to make any decision about you, your staff, your certification, or your account

## 3. If We Ever Introduce AI Features

We may in future add features that use AI — for example, adaptive practice questions or an assistant
that answers questions about course material. If we do:

- **We will update this policy and give account holders at least 30 days' notice before the change
  takes effect**
- **We will label AI-generated content within the Service** so it is never mistaken for
  human-authored material
- **We will not introduce AI into the scoring or certification decision** without saying so
  prominently and separately. The integrity of that path is the product
- **We will not begin training models on customer data** without express, opt-in written consent.
  Continued use of the Service will not be treated as consent

## 4. What We Teach About AI

The course covers the professional obligations that attach to AI use in legal practice, including:

- **Competence (ABA Model Rule 1.1)** — understanding the benefits and risks of the technology you use
- **Supervision (ABA Model Rule 5.3)** — supervising AI tools as you would a nonlawyer assistant, the
  obligation this Service exists to help document
- **Confidentiality (ABA Model Rule 1.6)** — taking reasonable steps to prevent disclosure of client
  information through AI tools
- **Verification** — the obligation to independently check AI output for accuracy, and the risk of
  fabricated citations and other plausible-but-false output

Guidance in this area is developing quickly. **The course is substantially updated each year**, which
is why certificates carry a 12-month validity period rather than being permanent. We encourage you to
consult your own state bar's current guidance, which may go further than the ABA Model Rules.

## 5. What This Training Does Not Do

Completing this training **does not**:

- Make you or your firm compliant with Rule 5.3, or any other rule. Compliance is a judgment about
  your whole practice, not a certificate
- Discharge, transfer, or reduce your supervisory obligations
- Constitute legal advice about your AI use, your tooling choices, or your firm's policies
- Certify that any particular AI tool is safe, appropriate, or approved for legal work. **We do not
  evaluate, endorse, or certify third-party AI products**

Training your staff is one supervisory step among several. Deciding what supervision your practice
requires, and carrying it out, remains yours.
