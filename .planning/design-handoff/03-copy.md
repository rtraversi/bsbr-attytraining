## Section 02 — Why Rule 5.3 just changed

**Heading:** Why Rule 5.3 just changed

**Body:**

> **ABA Model Rule 5.3** makes supervising attorneys responsible for the conduct of their nonlawyer
> staff. It was written for paralegals and office assistants — and in July 2024, **ABA Formal
> Opinion 512** extended it explicitly to AI tools.

> The practical consequence: if your paralegal uses an AI tool to draft a brief and it hallucinates
> citations, **you** are professionally responsible. You cannot blame the tool — and you cannot
> claim you didn't know.

**Pull quote:**
> "These duties apply to nonlawyers both within and outside of the law firm… a third-party-operated
> generative AI does not negate the lawyer's requirement to ensure that its actions are consistent
> with a lawyer's professional obligations."
>
> — Florida Bar Advisory Opinion 24-1 (2024)

> State bars in **Florida, California, New York, Texas, and North Carolina** have issued opinions
> explicitly tying Rule 5.3 to AI use. A completed training certificate is the "reasonable efforts
> to supervise" evidence the rule demands.

### Case list — three real sanctions

Presented as a docket, exhibit list, or card set — your call.

# LEGAL PAGES (`/privacy`, `/terms`, `/ai-policy`, `/accessibility`, `/dpa`)

One shared long-form template, five instances. Two of these routes do not exist yet.

**Structure per page:**
- Eyebrow: `IURIX`
- Title: "Privacy Policy" / "Terms of Service" / "AI Use Policy" / "Accessibility Statement" /
  "Data Processing Addendum"
- Meta line: "Last updated: [DATE]" or "Effective Date: [DATE]"
- Numbered sections — a heading plus one to several paragraphs, and in places sub-headings, bulleted
  lists, and simple tables
- Shared site footer

**Design the container, not the content** — full drafts exist and are under attorney review, so the
words will change but the shape will not. What matters: a comfortable measure (~65–75 characters),
clear section hierarchy down to two levels, and generous vertical rhythm.

**Plan for real length.** The Terms of Service runs to 19 top-level sections; the Privacy Policy to
11. At that length an in-page table of contents or sticky section navigation is worth proposing.
These pages are read carefully by exactly the sort of person who reads carefully.

Three elements the template must handle, which a simple prose template often misses:

1. **All-caps disclaimer blocks.** The Terms contain warranty, liability, and disclaimer paragraphs
   set in capitals — a legal convention for conspicuousness. They need a distinct treatment (a
   bordered or tinted block works) and must stay legible despite being all-caps.
2. **Simple tables.** Pricing bands, sub-processor lists, and contact details are tabular. They need
   a style, and they must scroll rather than overflow on mobile.
3. **A callout / "short version" block.** The AI Use Policy opens with a plain-language summary set
   apart from the body. It is the most quoted part of the document and should look deliberate.
