Why Rule 5.3 just changed

ABA Model Rule 5.3 makes supervising attorneys responsible for the conduct of their nonlawyer staff. It was written for paralegals and office assistants â€” and in July 2024, ABA Formal Opinion 512 extended it explicitly to AI tools.

The practical consequence: if your paralegal uses an AI tool to draft a brief and it hallucinates citations, you are professionally responsible. You cannot blame the tool â€” and you cannot claim you didn't know.

"These duties apply to nonlawyers both within and outside of the law firmâ€¦ a third-party-operated generative AI does not negate the lawyer's requirement to ensure that its actions are consistent with a lawyer's professional obligations." Florida Bar Advisory Opinion 24-1 (2024)

State bars in Florida, California, New York, Texas, and North Carolina have issued opinions explicitly tying Rule 5.3 to AI use. A completed training certificate is the "reasonable efforts to supervise" evidence the rule demands.
